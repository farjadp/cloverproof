## 1. Summary (for the CPO / COO)

1. Winter wheat is seeded in **fall** (late Aug to late Oct, depending on location) [S2, S6]. Phosphate is best drilled with the seed [S1], so P (and possibly some K) is decided **at fall seeding**, using a soil test usually taken after the previous crop's harvest [S3].
2. Fall N is **not recommended** in Ontario [S1, S5]. All N for yield is a **spring** decision.
3. Spring N goes on around **green-up to the start of stem elongation (Zadoks GS25–GS30)** [S7 citing Pub 811; S6]. In real years this has been **April to early May**: most fields had N+S by early May 2026 [S9, S10].
4. A second N pass, where used, lands at **GS32–GS39** [S4]. Splitting is conditional (late-planted / thin stands, or lodging-prone fields), not a default [S4, S1].
5. Ontario has **no calibrated soil nitrate test for winter wheat** in Pub 811; the nitrate test there is for spring barley [S1]. The ontario.ca nitrate sampling guidance is for corn [S3].
6. **Consequence for the pilot:** a Feb 1 – Apr 30, 2027 pilot fits the **spring N decision** (rate, split, timing) for wheat that is already in the ground. It is **too late** for the fall P/K-at-seeding decision of the 2027 crop; that would need a plan in Aug–Oct 2026 (already past) or belongs to the fall-2027 seeding of the 2028 crop. The plan must be **delivered before green-up in the farmer's area**, roughly by early-to-mid April in the south and later in the north and east [S9, S10] (calendar windows are UNVERIFIED as a rule; they come from two crop reports of a single year).

## 2. The season, step by step

### 2.1 Fall seeding (Aug–Oct)

- Pub 811 Chapter 4 has a map (Figure 4-4) of optimum seeding dates; the isolines are "based on average weather conditions from 1981 to 2017; actual results will vary from year to year" [S2].
- The Grain Farmers of Ontario staging guide (co-authored by an OMAFRA cereals specialist, reviewed by U of Guelph Ridgetown) prints optimum planting zones from **Zone M: Aug 17–21** (north) to **Zone A: Oct 16–20** (far south-west), in 5-day bands [S6, p. 61–62]. I could not confirm that this map is identical to Pub 811 Figure 4-4 (the Pub 811 map is an image I could not read). UNVERIFIED that they match.
- Seeding "more than 10 days before the optimum date introduces risk" from Hessian fly, snow mould and BYDV [S2].
- In practice seeding runs late: in fall 2024 "growers continued seeding well into late October" [S5].
- Pub 811 notes winter barley should be seeded 7–10 days before the wheat optimum [S2] (not relevant to the pilot crop; listed only because it is on the same page).

### 2.2 Fall fertility (at seeding)

- **Nitrogen:** "Application of fall nitrogen is discouraged under Ontario growing conditions" [S1]; "Fall applied N is **not** recommended in Ontario" [S5]. Pub 811 has a table (Table 4-16, "Fall applied nitrogen") comparing yield with all-spring N [S1]; I did not read the table values.
- **Phosphate:** "Where phosphate fertilizer is required for cereal crops, it is best drilled with the seed" [S1]. Pub 811 calls winter wheat "one of the most responsive crops to phosphorus fertilization" [S1]. Unincorporated fall-broadcast P can move off target [S1].
- **Potash:** Pub 811 says seed-placed fertilizer "may include some or all of the required nitrogen and potash, depending on rates of application" [S1]. I found **no Ontario source that states a separate timing for K on winter wheat** (fall broadcast vs with seed). UNVERIFIED.
- **Recommendations are based on OMAFRA-accredited soil tests** [S1]. P/K tables (Pub 811 Tables 4-20 and 4-21) have winter wheat columns [S1]; I did not read the values and they must not be copied into a pack without a human agronomist.
- **Sulphur:** fall sulphate leaches; spring sulphate is preferred [S1].
- **Manganese** (muck soils): severe-deficiency areas may need a fall application to ensure winter survival [S1].

### 2.3 When the soil test is taken

- ontario.ca: "If samples are taken immediately after harvest, the results will be back in time for planning the fertilizer program for the next crop." Sampling "once every three years is adequate" for most farms. Normal nutrient sampling depth about **15 cm**; nitrate sampling to **30 cm** [S3].
- For winter wheat, "immediately after harvest" of the previous crop (often soybean in Ontario rotations; not sourced here) means **late summer / early fall, right before wheat seeding**. The test that drives the P/K decision is therefore normally taken **before** the pilot window opens. Intake should ask for the test date and whether P/K was already applied at seeding.
- **Soil nitrate:** Pub 811's nitrate-nitrogen soil test section is titled for **spring barley** and samples "within 5 days of planting" to 30 cm [S1]. ontario.ca's nitrate guidance is pre-sidedress for **corn (V3–V4)** [S3]. I found **no OMAFA-calibrated soil nitrate test for winter wheat**. This matters for todo #9 (use of measured soil nitrate): there is no Ontario-published basis yet for a spring nitrate test in wheat. UNVERIFIED that none exists elsewhere in Pub 811.

### 2.4 Overwintering

- Winter wheat needs exposure to low temperatures (<10 °C) to initiate reproductive development (vernalization); tillering can happen in fall and spring [S6, p. 16].
- Winter survival risks: poor snow cover, freeze–thaw cycles, ice encasement, flooding at the crown, frost heaving [S6, p. 17].
- Regrowth "will occur after a week or two of warm weather, typically late April to early May, depending on the location and year" [S6, p. 17]. Note: crop reports show green-up earlier in the south (see 2.5), so this phrase is a general guide, not a date.
- Pub 811: "Assess the wheat crop during April and early May" and delay any replant decision as long as possible [S2].

### 2.5 Green-up and spring stand assessment

- Assess stands after a week or two of warm weather; count plants per foot of row at 10 spots; "5 healthy plants per foot of row is the minimum to maintain 80 per cent yield potential" (source in the guide: Smid, Ridgetown College, U of Guelph, 1986–90) [S6, p. 18–19].
- "If wheat was planted late or if the stand is thin, an application of nitrogen at green up can encourage tillering and canopy development" [S6, p. 18].
- In 2026: on Apr 23, fields in the north-central and eastern regions had "just greened up over the last week or two", while southern fields were already at GS30 [S10].
- **Pilot meaning:** the stand assessment at green-up can change the N plan (split or not, first-pass size). A plan delivered in February may need a revision at green-up. This is a new version (`parent_id` + reason), not an edit.

### 2.6 Spring N timing (Zadoks)

| Stage | Zadoks | What the source says |
|---|---|---|
| Tillering | GS21–29 (main shoot + 1…9+ tillers) | Stage definitions [S6, p. 2]. Pub 811: "Nitrogen and herbicide applications must be completed during the tillering stage" [S2]. |
| Green-up | not a Zadoks stage; plants usually GS2x | First N pass for late-planted or thin fields [S4, S6]. |
| Start of stem elongation | GS30 (pseudostem erect; head ~1/4" above crown) | "An important time for weed control, nitrogen applications" [S6, p. 20]. "GS 30 is important in winter wheat because this is when plants shift from vegetative to reproductive growth" [S8]. Single-pass timing in lodging-prone fields [S4]. |
| First / second node | GS31 / GS32 | |
| Flag leaf | GS37 / GS39 | Second pass of a split: "between GS32-39" [S4]. |

- Ontario standard (as cited by a U of Guelph MSc thesis, advisor D. Hooker): "apply N as the crop begins to green up in the spring, between GS25 and GS30 (OMAFRA, Publication 811)" [S7, p. 19]. The same thesis: "the bulk of N should be applied in late April to reduce N loss from wet soil conditions that may occur earlier" (citing OMAFRA, 2014) [S7, p. 20]. I could **not** find this sentence in the current online Pub 811 cereals fertility page [S1], whose "Timing of Nitrogen Application" subsection covers spring cereals only. Treat as UNVERIFIED against the current Pub 811 text.
- Advice is to time by stage, not calendar: "paying attention to growth stage rather than the calendar date is important" [S8]. In 2021 early-planted fields were at or near GS30 by mid-April [S8].
- 2026 calendar check: May 4–6, 2026 crop report: Exeter early-planted at GS32, later-planted just at GS30; Simcoe "generally at Zadok's growth stage 31 or 32"; "Most winter cereals have received an application of nitrogen and sulphur"; Simcoe "an estimated 90+% of winter wheat fields" had N [S9].
- N loss: "N losses through leaching and/or denitrification are more likely to occur with early-applied N" before rapid uptake at GS30; losses tend to be highest on sand and clay [S4].
- Hard red wheat for protein: "Nitrogen for grain protein production is required later in the development of the plant than the nitrogen for yield" [S1]; Pub 811 Table 4-15 ("Managing for increased protein") lists split timings GS30+32, GS30+69, GS30+32+69 [S1].

### 2.7 Single vs split N

From the OMAFRA cereals specialist article [S4] (2022):

- The answer is "sometimes…depends" on year, field and timing.
- **Late-planted / few tillers:** first pass at green-up; example split **60% N + 10 lb S** first, **40%** second (second at GS32–39). Waiting to GS30 can lose tillers if N is short.
- **Lodging-prone** (early planting, high seeding rate, susceptible variety, many tillers): delay first pass to GS30; example **40% N + 10 lb S** first, **60%** second at GS32–39.
- **Single pass:** "One pass means convenient, low application costs"; can give high yields if lodging and N loss are low.
- Trial: a later second pass at GS32 was significantly lower in 2011 and 2013 and significantly higher in 2012 (Table 1 in [S4]).
- Pub 811: "Ontario research data indicates that split N applications increase yield by 0.5%, on average" [S1].
- Cost: the 2022 example needed about 1.3 bu/ac more yield to pay for the extra pass [S4] (price-dependent; not a constant).

These percentages are **examples in one article**, not Ontario rules. They are not proposed as pack values. If the engine needs split fractions per scenario (todo #4), the pack entry must stay:

### 2.8 Other timing notes

- Sulphur: 10–15 kg/ha spring-applied sulphate or thiosulphate is Pub 811's stated optimum; "there is no predictive tool (soil test, tissue test) to determine responsive fields" [S1]. S usually goes on with the first N pass [S4, S9].
- Manure / legume sod: "Where manure is applied or the preceding crop is a legume sod, reduce the N rates" (Pub 811 Table 4-13 footnote) [S1]. No amount given there.
- Fungicide: with a T2 or T3 fungicide and no lodging concern, "spring N application rate may be increased by 30 kg N/ha" [S1]. Rate context only; not used here.

## 3. What this means for when farmers need a plan

| Decision | When it is made in Ontario | Source | In the Feb–Apr 2027 pilot? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seeding date, seeding rate | Aug–Oct (by zone) | S2, S6 | No (2027 crop already seeded in fall 2026) |
| P (and any K) for the wheat crop | At fall seeding, P drilled with seed; soil test taken after previous crop's harvest | S1, S3 | No for the 2027 crop. Pilot can **record** what was applied and flag low-testing fields for the next seeding. |
| Fall N | Not recommended | S1, S5 | n/a |
| Spring N: total rate | Before green-up | S1, S7 | **Yes, core of the pilot** |
| Spring N: split or single, first-pass timing | At/after green-up stand assessment (GS2x–GS30) | S4, S6 | **Yes**; may need a revised version after the stand check |
| Second N pass (if split) | GS32–GS39 | S4 | Yes; for southern fields this may fall in early May, after Apr 30 (S9). The SOP's May 7 final log date may be tight for split fields. UNVERIFIED as a general rule. |
| Sulphur | Spring, with first N pass | S1, S4 | Yes |

Implications for `docs/operations/pilot-sop.md` and `docs/operations/agronomist-workload-model.md` (for the human agronomist to confirm; no change made by me):

1. **Deadline per farm = before green-up in that area**, not Apr 30. In 2026 the south was at GS30 by Apr 23, while the north and east had only just greened up [S10]. The crunch is therefore likely **March to early April**, earlier in the south-west. (Single-year evidence; UNVERIFIED as a rule.)
2. **Plans are two-stage:** a February/March plan (total spring N, intended split), then a possible **green-up revision** after the stand count. Workload should count a second review for some fields.
3. **P/K is out of season** for the 2027 crop. Plans without a soil test are N-only anyway (CLAUDE.md rule 3); for fields with a test, P/K output is for the record and for the next seeding, unless the human agronomist decides spring P/K top-dress on winter wheat is in scope (no Ontario source found for this; UNVERIFIED).
4. **The fall decision point** (Aug–Oct) is a second pilot window for a future season (2027 seeding for the 2028 crop): soil test after previous-crop harvest → P/K at seeding → spring N plan. Not in current scope; flagged for the CPO.
5. **Soil nitrate:** do not ask farmers for a spring soil nitrate test on wheat as a requirement; no Ontario-calibrated use found (see 2.3, todo #9).
