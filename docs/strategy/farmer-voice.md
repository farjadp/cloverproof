# Farmer voice: how Ontario and Prairie grain growers think about fertilizer decisions (public sources)

Owner: market-research-analyst for cso. Date: 2026-11-17. Status: draft, desk research only. Skill used: `customer-research` (public-source mode).
Labels: FACT (cited), HYPOTHESIS (ours), UNKNOWN. All access dates 2026-11-17.

## Method and limits
- Read-only. No contact, accounts, posts, logins or paywalled text. Nothing here is a farmer quote: paraphrases restate what a source says, with no names, farms or locations finer than province.
- **Biggest limit:** public sources are mostly extension staff, associations, journalists and agronomists speaking about growers, not growers speaking. Genuine grower voice is thin. Treat every theme as a lead for interviews, not a finding.
- Some pages were read in full; others only as search-result summaries (marked "snippet"). Snippet-only claims need a re-read before anyone quotes them externally.
- No agronomic rates are advised or implied here. Numbers cited are the sources' own.
- Our pricing is not discussed. Willingness-to-pay evidence is nearly absent (theme 9).

## Themes

### 1. 2026 fertilizer prices and supply scared growers more than any agronomy question
- **Evidence:** 5 independent sources (GFO, Ridgetown survey, Farms.com, FCC, Manitoba Co-operator). Ridgetown input survey (n unknown): urea averaged $856/t in April 2026 vs $657 in October 2025 (snippet). Mostly corn/N, thin on winter wheat.
- **Paraphrases:** The provincial grain body told federal ministers that N prices jumped 65% almost overnight, that some retailers had only about 60% of usual supply, and that growers cannot plant what they cannot afford to fertilize. Trade press reported modest N cuts of roughly 5-10% as a margin-protection move (corn-focused, snippet). A Prairie columnist argued growers cannot control price but can control how much they buy.
- **Sources:** [GFO letter, 2026-03-06](https://gfo.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-03-06-Urgent-Action-re-Fertilizer-Crisis.pdf); [Ridgetown farm input survey, 2026-04-15](https://www.ridgetownc.com/documents/ontario-farm-input-monitoring-project-survey-1-apr-15-2026-pdf/); [Farms.com](https://www.farms.com/ag-industry-news/middle-east-conflict-pushes-fertilizer-costs-higher-forcing-ontario-growers-to-rethink-corn-acres-352.aspx) (snippet); [FCC outlook 2027](https://www.farms.com/news/fertilizer-outlook-mixed-heading-into-2027-fcc-246317.aspx) (snippet); [Manitoba Co-operator](https://www.manitobacooperator.ca/?p=238544).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** price volatility makes "justify this rate" a live pain; message on rate defensibility and record, never on "save money" (no savings promise per positioning). Test whether the pain fades when prices ease (FCC says N easing, P tight for 2027).

### 2. Winter wheat N is a season-by-season judgment (stage, planting date, variety, soil), not a lookup
- **Evidence:** 4 sources, all extension/trial-side (OMAFRA cereal specialist, Ridgetown trials, Field Crop News, Farms.com). No grower survey found.
- **Paraphrases:** An OMAFRA specialist says splitting N helps in some conditions (late-planted or thin stands, high loss risk) but trials show the benefit varies by year and a single pass is cheaper and simpler. Ridgetown trials report that planting date and variety moved lodging and yield more than extra N or growth regulators. Field Crop News crop reports track N+S going on in April, about 10% of acres by mid-April and most by early May 2026.
- **Sources:** [OGF split-apply N, 2022](https://ontariograinfarmer.ca/2022/03/01/split-apply-nitrogen/); [OGF wheat intensity, 2026](https://ontariograinfarmer.ca/2026/04/01/pushing-the-intensity-on-wheat-production/); [Field Crop News crop conditions 2026-04-13](https://fieldcropnews.com/2026/04/crop-conditions-week-of-april-13-2026/) (snippet); [Farms.com OMAFRA reminders](https://www.farms.com/news/winter-wheat-fertilizer-application-reminders-121205.aspx) (snippet).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** the "when" and "split or not" questions matter as much as the "how much"; the Feb-Apr window is short, so the product must be fast. Risk: if planting date and variety dominate, an N-only tool may feel narrow.

### 3. Growers believe they follow good practice more than data shows; most have no formal plan
- **Evidence:** 3 outlets reporting one primary survey: Fertilizer Canada 2024 Fertilizer Use Survey, n=1,258, online Nov 2024-Mar 2025, mostly Western crops. 64-65% believe they follow 4R; about 28% of cropland (about 13M acres) meets criteria; nearly 80% have no formal nutrient plan. Self-reported, not third-party validated.
- **Paraphrase:** Press summarising the survey says the main obstacle to "right rate" is that many growers do not set field-specific N rates and fall back on generic recommendations.
- **Sources:** [Western Producer](https://www.producer.com/crops/4r-nutrient-stewardship-compliance-comes-up-short/); [Manitoba Co-operator](https://www.manitobacooperator.ca/?p=238544); [Fertilizer Canada report PDF](https://fertilizercanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Fertilizer-Use-Survey-National-Report-2024.pdf) (could not be machine-read; figures via press).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** a confidence-and-proof record sells to the gap between "I think I do 4R" and "I can show it". Do not claim 4R certification. Ontario-only split of these figures: unknown.

### 4. Soil testing: widely endorsed, unevenly practised, judged by whether it changes the plan
- **Evidence:** 4 sources. Survey: 61% test N every 1-3 years; the rest every 4-5 years, less or never (Fertilizer Canada, n=1,258). A Co-operator columnist reads that as only about a quarter testing every year.
- **Paraphrases:** Ontario guidance says grid sampling only pays if results change what is applied. Trade press describes soil tests as costing hundreds of dollars, which some growers see as a drain, and a common habit of applying at removal rates without a test. Retailers now typically bill sampling separately (snippet).
- **Sources:** [Western Producer](https://www.producer.com/crops/4r-nutrient-stewardship-compliance-comes-up-short/); [OGF asset or liability: soil fertility, 2021](https://ontariograinfarmer.ca/2021/02/01/asset-or-liability-soil-fertility/); [Manitoba Co-operator](https://www.manitobacooperator.ca/?p=238544); [Ontario.ca soil sampling](https://www.ontario.ca/page/soil-sampling-and-analysis-managing-crop-nutrients) (snippet); [OGF](https://ontariograinfarmer.ca/?p=8533) (snippet).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** our "no test: N only, test recommended for P/K" rule matches current habits; a pilot grower with a stale or no test is normal, not an edge case. Confidence labels can make the cost of skipping a test visible.

### 5. Trust in advice is relationship-based; retailer conflict of interest is a recurring worry, independent advice is trusted but rarely paid for
- **Evidence:** 3 sources, mostly US/general; no Ontario or Prairie trust survey found. A Mosaic-commissioned survey of 400+ North American growers reportedly placed independent consultants highest for fertility decisions (snippet; figures unverified).
- **Paraphrases:** Critics say retail recommendations come from parties who benefit from the sale. Retailers answer that long-term relationships make bad advice unprofitable. GFO commentary says soil maps are only as good as the farmer-agronomist relationship. Independent third-party sampling firms are described as growing.
- **Sources:** [UCS commentary](https://blog.ucs.org/guest-commentary/ask-a-scientist-are-farmers-wasting-money-on-fertilizer/) (snippet); [Precision Farming Dealer](https://precisionfarmingdealer.com/blogs/1-from-the-virtual-terminal/post/3048-is-the-call-out-justified) (snippet); [CropNutrition third-party sampling](https://www.cropnutrition.com/resource-library/third-party-soil-sampling-what-you-need-to-know/) (snippet); [OGF](https://ontariograinfarmer.ca/?p=8533) (snippet).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** "vendor-neutral" resonates as a concept but growers may not leave a trusted dealer; position as a second opinion that keeps the dealer relationship, not a replacement. Never imply dealers are dishonest.

### 6. Data ownership and sharing: broad discomfort, strongest for raw production data and for sharing with tech/service providers
- **Evidence:** 4 sources, 3 surveys. A cross-sector Canadian survey, n about 1,000 (2021, presented 2025): 44% uncomfortable sharing with tech/service providers, only 16% think producers benefit most from aggregated data, weather/soil data easiest to share, input/production data hardest, Saskatchewan more uneasy than Quebec. FCC survey of 2,000+ producers: 25% less comfortable sharing with outside organisations over two years (snippet). Guelph study: 83% privacy, 89% ownership, 87% sharing concerns (snippet; years likely 2018-19).
- **Paraphrases:** Many growers do not know what their contracts say about data. Some say they would give up ownership if compensated. Research institutions are the most trusted recipients, though not by all.
- **Sources:** [Western Producer, trust failures study](https://www.producer.com/news/new-study-flags-trust-failures-with-big-farm-data/); [Farms.com, FCC survey](https://www.farms.com/news/survey-shows-farmers-cautious-about-sharing-farm-data-143246.aspx) (snippet; direct fetch failed); [Western Producer 2018](https://www.producer.com/2018/12/the-wild-west-of-agricultural-data/) (snippet).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** a plain, grower-owned, shareable-on-the-grower's-choice record is a differentiator; the data terms should be one page and shown early. Whether this changes purchase: unknown.

### 7. Tool fatigue is mostly about interoperability, usability and cost, not about software per se
- **Evidence:** 4 sources, no recent grower-level survey. 2017 AAFC barrier ranking, 261 answers: price, connectivity, lack of knowledgeable people, fast-changing tech, old equipment. Software use reported to have doubled from about one-third (2017) to two-thirds (2024) of farmers (Stratus, snippet).
- **Paraphrases:** Growers do not want several apps that do not talk to each other. Producers want tools as simple as social media apps. Older operators near retirement see little payoff.
- **Sources:** [Western Producer, digital ag barriers, 2023](https://www.producer.com/crops/digital-ag-delivers-but-barriers-remain/); [Country Guide, Prairie tech](https://www.country-guide.ca/?p=88069); [CAPI digital agriculture 2025](https://capi-icpa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-05-20-Digital-Agriculture-EN-CAPI.pdf) (snippet); [Western Producer, data governance and ROI](https://researchmoneyinc.com/article/concerns-about-data-governance-returns-on-investment-are-slowing-adoption-of-digital-technologies-in-agriculture) (paywalled past the first paragraph; not used beyond it).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** concierge delivery (PDF, no app to install) fits this; avoid adding a login-heavy app in the pilot. Export-friendly output matters more than integrations in year 1.

### 8. Paperwork: regulatory records are tolerated only where mandatory; growers want less re-entry
- **Evidence:** 3 sources, all organisation submissions, not growers; the two cited burden submissions come from OFA (general) and Beef Farmers of Ontario, not wheat growers.
- **Paraphrases:** Ontario nutrient-management records apply only to operations needing a strategy or plan. OFA objected to five-year plan renewals for unchanged operations and suggested pre-filling records. BFO asked for templates and alignment with dealer record standards. An inspector says missing records are among the most common infractions found.
- **Sources:** [Ontario.ca recordkeeping](https://www.ontario.ca/page/farms-nutrient-management-strategies-or-plans-recordkeeping-requirements); [OFA ERO submission](https://ofa.on.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/ERO-Nutrient-Management.pdf); [BFO submission 2021](https://www.ontariobeef.com/media/5ahc2jnc/2021_07_20-bfo-submission-ero-019-3849-nma-further-burden-reduction-opportunities.pdf); [OGF](https://ontariograinfarmer.ca/?p=911) (snippet).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** a proof record is not a regulatory document (positioning excludes NMP sign-off); frame it as "decision file" that reduces re-entry, not as compliance. Most winter wheat growers without livestock may have no mandatory N record: unknown, check in interviews.

### 9. Willingness to pay for advice or software: effectively unknown
- **Evidence:** 0 sources with a measured figure for Canadian grain growers. Related: positioning.md cites Canola Council that most growers do not pay for independent agronomy (link returned 404 on re-check; treat as unverified). Guelph work suggests some would trade data for free software (snippet). Price is a top barrier (2017 AAFC).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** assume price-sensitive in 2026 margin conditions; the pilot is the first real evidence. Test price only with real offers in interviews, never in public material.

### 10. Official N guidance for small cereals is seen by the grower body as needing refresh
- **Evidence:** 1 source. GFO June 2026 research priorities include data to support updates to Ontario nutrient rate recommendations, emphasising N in wheat (snippet). This is the association's priority, not a grower survey.
- **Sources:** [GFO research priorities 2026](https://gfo.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026-06-10-2026-GFO-Research-Priorities-FINAL-BOARD-APPROVED.pdf) (snippet).
- **Implication (HYPOTHESIS):** a vendor-neutral tool built on OMAFA rules must say clearly which basis each number rests on; the agronomist-edit dataset may become valuable precisely because official guidance is under review. Do not claim our numbers are better than official ones.

## Contradictions and what we did not find
- Grower self-image vs data: 64-65% say they follow 4R, about 28% meet it (theme 3). Self-report may overstate in both directions.
- Soil-test frequency is read as "61% test regularly" (Fertilizer Canada) and as "only a quarter test yearly" (columnist); the definitions differ (every 1-3 years vs yearly).
- Retailer conflict claims come from US critics; retailers dispute them. No Ontario or Prairie measure.
- Rate-cut evidence is corn-focused; winter wheat grower response to 2026 prices: unknown.
- Not found: a grower-level Ontario winter wheat survey on N decisions; Ontario-only split of the Fertilizer Canada data; any figure on what growers pay for independent advice or software; grower views on confidence labels or versioned records; Manitoba/Prairie wheat-specific N-decision voice (Prairie evidence is canola-heavy); Farmtario and Real Agriculture pages (only a mirror of one RealAg item surfaced).
- Fertilizer Canada national report PDF could not be read by the tool; figures come from press coverage of it.

## Interview questions for the pilot (one or more per theme)
1. (T1) When you set this spring's N rate, what changed compared with last year, and who or what did you consult? Would the same change happen if prices fell?
2. (T2) Walk me through your last winter wheat N decision: dates, stage, split or single pass, and what would have made you change it.
3. (T2) Which matters more to you, N rate, planting date or variety? Where do you want help?
4. (T3) Do you have a written nutrient or fertility plan? Who wrote it, when did you last open it?
5. (T3) Could you show someone, a lender, buyer or advisor, why you chose last year's rate? Where is that written?
6. (T4) When did you last soil test this field, who sampled, what did it cost, and did the result change anything you did?
7. (T4) If we told you "low confidence, no recent test", what would you do?
8. (T5) Who do you trust most for a second opinion on rates? Would you share a dealer's recommendation with a neutral tool?
9. (T5) What would make you leave your dealer's plan, and what would never make you leave?
10. (T6) Who would you let see your field records? What terms would you need in writing before uploading anything?
11. (T7) What apps or portals do you already use for fields? What would a new one need to replace?
12. (T7) Would a PDF by email be enough, or do you need a login?
13. (T8) Which records are you required to keep, which do you keep anyway, and which do you re-type for others?
14. (T9) What do you pay today for sampling, agronomy or software, per year or per acre? What would you pay for this and under what conditions? (Collect, do not anchor.)
15. (T10) Do you follow the provincial recommendations as published, adjust them, or ignore them, and why?
