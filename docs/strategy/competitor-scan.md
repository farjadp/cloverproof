## 2. Where paying could make sense (RK-08)

**What the evidence supports**

1. **The free calculation is not a decision.** AgriSuite outputs needs and removal; a single P/K test supports several defensible answers, and the grower chooses (FCN 2025-07-10). Wheat N: the published guide may lag current fungicide-era management (GFO project page), and no public wheat N calculator was found. A single reasoned, confidence-labelled number is a different product from a calculator. Willingness to pay for it is untested.
2. **Someone other than the grower pays for plans.** OFCAF Nitrogen Management covers 65% (75% for new applicants), capped at $30,000 per project, and third-party Crop Nutrient Plan preparation is an eligible cost (snippet of OSCIA pages; program status "not accepting"/"upcoming" conflicts across sources, so verify before relying on it). Funded programs create a document requirement; a document producer can sit inside that budget (ESTIMATE).
3. **Documentation gap exists in 4R.** Designated 4R agronomists sign off plans and acre reports; retailers are audited on documentation; yet only 17% of farmers hold a formal plan (Fertilizer Canada, 2024 survey). Intermediaries (4R retailers, CCAs) must produce and retain plan records; a multi-farm, versioned, hash-verified record serves them directly (ESTIMATE).
4. **Price anchors are low-to-moderate.** Software with records and scripting starts around $400-500 per year (FieldView, AgExpert); Prairie consulting runs $4-10/ac. Free alternatives (AgriSuite, FE Soils, lab recs) cap what a grower pays for the number alone, near zero (ESTIMATE).
5. **Conflict-of-interest wedge.** Retail agronomy is bundled with product sales and not vendor-neutral. A neutral second opinion has value to growers and to advisors who want to show independence (ESTIMATE; no public survey found).

**Where it fails**

- No buyer, lender, or insurer requiring wheat nutrient records was found. GFO publishes principles for sustainability programs but I found no wheat nutrient-data program in Ontario. Third-party demand for records is UNKNOWN.
- Winter wheat is one main spring N decision per field per year; usage frequency is low (ESTIMATE). A subscription needs more touchpoints (log, multiple crops).
- OMAFRA can add a report, a wheat N calculator, or Crop Nutrient Plan templates for free; AgriSuite is already used by OMAFRA staff reviewing plans.
- The reviewer is an AI agent; no named human agronomist. Programs, 4R sign-off, and CCAs rely on a qualified person. Until one is attached we cannot state "agronomist-reviewed", and 4R-style sign-off is out of reach (ESTIMATE).
- Retailers give advice and prescriptions as part of selling product; a grower may see no reason to pay for a second one.
- Our confidence rules (no soil test: N only, medium max) are more conservative than free tools and may feel like less output to the user.

## 3. Pilot hypotheses (Feb-Apr 2027, 5-10 farms)

| H1 | The proof PDF gets used outside the farm. | Farms that share the PDF with an advisor, retailer, lender, insurer, or program, within the pilot (log who, why). | At least 50% of farms, and at least 2 distinct recipient types. |
